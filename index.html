    Why e-waste matters

    Electronics are the fastest-growing household waste stream. Source: UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024.

    62 Mt

    of e-waste generated worldwide in 2022, about 7.8 kg for every person.

    22.3%

    was documented as properly collected and recycled. The rest was lost, burned or landfilled.

    82 Mt

    projected for 2030 if recycling does not keep pace with consumption.

    Toxic materials

    Lead, mercury and cadmium leach into soil and water when devices are dumped.

    Recoverable value

    Phones and boards hold copper, gold, silver and cobalt that can be mined again from scrap.

    Fire risk

    Damaged lithium batteries can ignite in bins and trucks. Keep them out of household trash.

    Recycling tips

    1. Back up and factory-reset phones and laptops before handing them over.
    2. Tape the terminals of loose batteries and store them in a dry place.
    3. Ask the maker or retailer about take-back programs first.
    4. Use only authorized recyclers that can show certification.
    5. Repair or donate working devices before recycling them.
